# Prestige Cheshire Detailing: homepage concept and free site audit

Prepared by Integrate · 24 November 2026

## 1. Project summary

- **Business:** Prestige Cheshire Detailing, a mobile car detailer run by Craig. It's on Facebook as *prestigecheshirevaleting*.
- **What they do:** machine polishing to remove swirl marks, and Gyeon ceramic coatings (mostly the 5-year coating). They work on cars, vans and motorbikes, fully mobile with their own water and power, by appointment only.
- **Areas:** Chester, Northwich, Winsford, Sandbach, Crewe, Nantwich, Macclesfield, Wilmslow, Bramhall.
- **Goal of the new homepage:** turn people who find Craig online into WhatsApp conversations.
- **Primary conversion action:** WhatsApp Craig (pre-filled message). The secondary action is calling 07791 543486.

## 2. Free site audit

### What is working today
- **Reputation.** 100% of 21 Facebook reviewers recommend the business. Reviewers mention Craig by name and say he replies quickly, turns up on time and talks them through the job. That is rare, valuable proof. *(Source: the business's Facebook page, as supplied.)*
- **The work itself.** The Facebook photos show genuinely high-end cars: a Porsche Macan, Range Rover, Range Rover Sport, BMW M2, an Audi RS-style estate, BMW 3 Series and X3, and a black VW Transporter. The finishes are glossy and reflective. This is the best sales material the business has.
- **Clear specialism.** Polishing and Gyeon coatings is a premium niche, not a "£10 wash" offer.
- **WhatsApp is already set up** on Facebook, which makes enquiring low-friction.

### What is holding them back
- **There is no working website.** prestigecheshiredetailing.co.uk is linked from Facebook, but it returns an HTTP 404 page saying "Web space not ready" on desktop and mobile. *(Checked 24 Nov 2026. Evidence: `qa/evidence/current-site-desktop.png`, `qa/evidence/current-site-mobile.png`.)* Anyone who clicks through from Facebook, or types the address from a van or card, hits a dead end.
- **There are no Lighthouse scores for the current site** because there's no site to test. Every visitor who reaches the domain is lost.
- **A different "Prestige" business owns the old name online.** prestigevaletingcheshire.co.uk is a live site for "Prestige Valeting". It has a different phone number (07415 110597), a different email and budget valeting prices ("from £5.00"). It also links to a Trustpilot profile showing about 11 reviews and a TrustScore of about 4.5. *(Checked 24 Nov 2026.)* We could not confirm any link between it and Craig, and the different phone number suggests it's a separate business. Either way, someone searching "Prestige valeting Cheshire" can land there instead of on Craig. **To confirm with Craig.**
- **Everything lives on Facebook.** Facebook pages are hard to find in Google for service searches. They don't explain the process or answer price, area or timing questions, and they can't rank a page for each town.

## 4. Missed opportunities, and what they are likely costing

- **Searches with buying intent are going to competitors and directories:** "ceramic coating [town]", "paint correction [town]", "swirl removal near me", "mobile detailer [town]", and "van detailing / Transit Custom ceramic coating". Craig covers nine towns but has no page for any of them.
- **Lost click-throughs from his own Facebook.** Every "website" tap from Facebook currently ends on a 404.
- **Name confusion.** People who remember "Prestige" and search for it can end up with a different valeting company and phone number.
- **Estimate (clearly an estimate):** ceramic coating and correction jobs are high-value. If the missing searches and the dead link cost even one or two coating jobs a month, that is likely to be the business's biggest single leak. We haven't seen Craig's prices or volumes, so we can't put a number on it.

## 6. The new homepage

### Concept
*The red line under the car in Craig's logo becomes the horizon of the whole site.* It's the reflection running along a freshly polished panel, and it leads the visitor from dull, swirled paint to deep gloss, then into a WhatsApp chat with Craig. It fits because it's built from Craig's own logo and Craig's own photos. A competitor couldn't reskin it without both.

### Scroll journey
1. **Hero.** Macro water beading on black paint with a red light streak, and a highlight that follows your cursor. The headline is "Paint you can see yourself in." Within one screen: polishing and Gyeon coatings, cars, vans and bikes, Cheshire, own water and power. The WhatsApp CTA and the phone number in full sit alongside a "100% recommend · 21 Facebook reviews" chip.
2. **Why paint looks tired.** "Most paint isn't dull. It's scratched." The explanation lights up word by word as you scroll.
3. **Signature moment: The Polish Pass.** A sticky scroll scene built on Craig's own photo of a black Range Rover panel, covered in a drawn layer of swirl marks. The panel is clearly labelled "Illustration · swirls added digitally". As you scroll, a rotary polisher pad makes overlapping passes and erases the swirls, revealing the true reflection. A live "polish progress" counter runs from 0% to 100%, and the stages advance: Prep → Machine polish → Gyeon ceramic coating. Your cursor or finger becomes a **swirl-finder light**, the same inspection torch detailers use. Under it, any swirls left on the panel flare up, just as they do under a real inspection light. Scroll back and the swirls return. When it finishes: "Get this for your car" (WhatsApp).
4. **The garage.** A horizontal, pinned passage of real customer cars at full height. Hover shows a second angle. It ends with "Send Craig a photo of yours."
5. **Two things, done properly.** Machine polishing and Gyeon ceramic coatings, with Craig's close-up photos. Then a "Not sure which you need?" WhatsApp prompt.
6. **Vans.** "Your van is your shop window." The black Transporter, with Transit Customs mentioned.
7. **Coverage.** "We bring the water. And the power." The nine towns are plotted from real coordinates, joined by a red route that draws as you scroll. Own water, own power, appointment only.
8. **Proof.** A huge 100%, "21 Facebook reviews. Every one recommends.", and the three things reviewers mention. These are written as themes, not invented quotes, with a link to the real reviews.
9. **Good questions.** Price, water and power, areas, coating lifespan, vans and bikes, booking.
10. **Climax: "Let's get it booked in."** A studio stage with a red neon horizon, and a three-tap builder (vehicle, service, town) that writes the WhatsApp message for the visitor.
11. **Footer.** A giant outlined PRESTIGE wordmark with the red line drawn under it.

### How it converts
- WhatsApp is one tap away at all times: a header pill on desktop, and a fixed thumb bar on mobile (WhatsApp + Call).
- There are action points at every decision moment: hero, after the polish pass, end of the garage, services, vans, coverage, FAQ and the final builder.
- Objections are answered before the final CTA: price (send photos for a quote), trust (100% of 21, Craig named), area (map), logistics (own water and power), timing (appointment only).
- No animation hides contact details. The hero intro runs in CSS from first paint, and every CTA is a real link.

## 8. Brand system used

| Role | Hex |
|---|---|
| Background (black) | `#08080A` |
| Surface (ink) | `#0F1013` |
| Surface (graphite) | `#17181C` |
| Text (chrome white) | `#F2F1ED` |
| Secondary text (mist) | `#A3A5AB` |
| Tertiary text (dim) | `#6B6D73` |
| Accent (logo red) | `#E3120B` |
| Accent hover / glow | `#FF2A1F` |
| Hairlines | `rgba(242,241,237,0.12)` |

- **Type:** Archivo (variable, width axis). Headlines use the widest setting (125), weight 800, uppercase and tight tracking for an engineered, coachbuilt feel. Body copy uses width 100. JetBrains Mono is used for spec labels and eyebrows.
- **Logo:** redrawn as a vector (`components/Icons.tsx` → `LogoMark`, and `public/mark.svg`): black disc, white car outline, red line beneath.
- **Motion principles:** expo-out easing, no linear tweens except scroll-scrubs, motion that follows the light (reflections, sweeps, the red line), scroll that controls rather than triggers, and a full `prefers-reduced-motion` fallback (no pins, static finished states).

## 9. Tech stack and quality

- Next.js 15 (App Router), React 19, TypeScript, Tailwind v4 and hand-written CSS tokens.
- GSAP + ScrollTrigger (scrubs) and Lenis smooth scroll, code-split and loaded on the visitor's first interaction. The pinned scenes are CSS `position: sticky`, so there are no layout shifts.
- A canvas-based Polish Pass with procedurally generated swirl marks and a pointer-driven swirl-finder light.
- An IntersectionObserver image loader and WebP images at 800w and 1400w. **Customer number plates are pixelated** in every photo.
- `noindex, nofollow` in the meta tag and an `X-Robots-Tag` header.
- **Lighthouse mobile (local production build, final):** performance 94, accessibility 100, best practices 100. LCP is 3.0s, TBT 70ms, CLS 0.013. SEO is intentionally low because of noindex.
- **Layout shift while scrolling the whole page:** 0.018 on desktop and 0.012 on mobile.

### Critique rounds
- **Round 1.**
  - Problems found: the swirl "before" state was too subtle; the hero headline wrapped awkwardly; the proof % was detached; the footer wordmark was clipped; the van heading was oversized; one unverifiable claim ("No upsell menu").
  - Fixes: a stronger haze and denser swirls, wider hero columns, the claim removed.
- **Round 2.**
  - Problems found: map labels collided with the route; services read as flat; the mobile garage had spacing issues.
  - Fixes: labels repositioned, services restyled to an alternating wide-display layout, and the swirl-finder light added to the signature moment.
- **Round 3 (performance).**
  - Problem found: Lighthouse mobile performance was 62.
  - Fixes: the hero intro moved to CSS so text is never hidden by JS; GSAP and Lenis were code-split; viewport-based image loading was added; a lighter mobile hero image was used.
  - Result: performance went from 62 to 85–87.
- **Independent quality gate rounds 1–3:** see section 12.
